\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Probe-based early exit for reasoning.}
The methods we scrutinize periodically query a partial reasoning trajectory for
its current answer and halt on agreement. Dynasor / Certaindex
\citep{dynasor_certaindex} formalize this with a ``certainty index'' over
recent probe answers and use it to schedule early termination when serving
reasoning programs; related work targets the \emph{overthinking} of reasoning
models \citep{overthinking} and proposes dynamic early exit
\citep{deer}. Our study is built on a fork of this line of work: we adopt the
same probe mechanism (a short boxed-answer suffix) and the same notion of
probe agreement, and ask how far \emph{any} rule in this family can be pushed
before it becomes unsafe.

\paragraph{Early-stopping self-consistency.}
The nearest neighbors to our setting stop \emph{self-consistency}
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency} early: Adaptive-Consistency
\citep{aggarwal2023adaptive} and Early-Stopping Self-Consistency
\citep{li2024esc} halt sampling once a majority among \emph{completed} samples
is statistically settled. Probe-based early exit is the online,
\emph{single-trajectory} analogue: it votes across snapshots of one in-progress
trajectory rather than across finished samples. This difference is exactly why
their stopping criteria do not transfer---snapshots of an unfinished trajectory
are not exchangeable draws from a stable answer distribution, because the
trajectory is still correcting itself (Section~\ref{sec:falseconsensus}).

\paragraph{Self-correction.}
Our ``recovery'' statistic---most wrong intermediate answers are later
overturned---is a form of intrinsic self-correction, but it differs from the
prompted, post-hoc self-correction that \citet{huang2024selfcorrect} find
unreliable: we measure answer evolution \emph{within} a single uninterrupted
trajectory, not a second corrective pass. Our result is complementary: even
when a model is not asked to self-correct, its mid-trajectory answer is not yet
final.

\paragraph{Test-time scaling and verification.}
A large literature trades inference compute for accuracy via repeated sampling
and search \citep{snell2024scaling,brown2024monkeys}; early exit is the dual
problem of \emph{spending less} without losing accuracy. Process reward models
and verifier-guided decoding \citep{lightman2024verify,wang2024mathshepherd}
score partial reasoning and are a natural source of a termination signal; we
return to this in Section~\ref{sec:discussion} as an alternative to
consensus-based stopping.

\paragraph{Adaptive computation and token-level early exit.}
Adaptive computation time \citep{graves2016act} and confident adaptive language
modeling \citep{schuster2022confident} let models spend variable compute per
input via a learned or thresholded halting signal at the \emph{token or layer}
level. Answer-level early exit for reasoning operates at the much coarser
granularity of a whole answer and must contend with non-monotone answer
trajectories; we quantify exactly this non-monotonicity.

\paragraph{Confidence and calibration.}
That model confidence overstates correctness is well documented for classifiers
\citep{guo2017calibration} and studied for LLMs via self-reported confidence
\citep{kadavath2022know} and semantic uncertainty \citep{kuhn2023semantic}. We
report an analogous miscalibration for \emph{probe consensus}: the share of
recent probes agreeing on an answer is a poorly calibrated estimate of that
answer's correctness, and is worst precisely in the regime a stopping rule would
act on.

\paragraph{Preregistration.}
To keep an exhaustive rule search from silently overfitting to a favorable
operating point, we fix the rule schema, splits, and acceptance gates before
looking at held-out data \citep{preregistration_ml}. To our knowledge this is
among the first preregistered evaluations of early-exit rules for reasoning
models; the protocol (Section~\ref{sec:method}) makes a \emph{negative} result
interpretable.
