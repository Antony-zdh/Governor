\section{Results: No Safe-and-Saving Stop in the Searched Space}
\label{sec:results}

We aggregate the sweep into per-environment metrics---$637{,}632$ rows
$=17{,}712$ rules $\times$ $18$ environments $\times$ $2$ splits (train, dev)---
and compute, for each rule, its worst per-model and per-benchmark accuracy drop
and its net token savings (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} defines the accounting).
All numbers below are on the development set unless noted; held-out confirmation
is reported in the final subsection. Applying commitment~C2 to the preregistered
conservative gate yields the paper's central result.

\subsection{The conservative gate is empty}
\textbf{No rule in the $17{,}712$-candidate space clears the conservative gate}
(per-model drop $\le 1.5\pp$, per-benchmark $\le 2.0\pp$, psf $\ge 0.8$). The
Pareto frontier over (worst-case drop, net savings) contains $93$ rules, and
none is admissible. The obstruction is not a narrow miss:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{smallest} per-model accuracy drop anywhere in the space is
  $1.85\pp$ (a pooled per-model quantity, not a single-problem artifact;
  Section~\ref{sec:resolution})---already above the $1.5\pp$ gate---and the rules
  achieving it have \emph{negative} net savings (20th-percentile saving
  $\approx -11\%$; only $2.8\%$ of environments save at all);
  \item \emph{no} rule achieves a near-zero drop: over all $17{,}712$ candidate
  rules the per-model drop has percentiles $p_1{=}3.37$, $p_5{=}4.26$,
  $p_{25}{=}10.7$, median $20.1\pp$;
  \item obtaining even three distinct rules with \emph{positive} net savings
  requires accepting a per-model drop of at least $4.87\pp$---and this holds
  whether the psf floor is set to $0.8$, $0.7$, $0.6$, or $0.5$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Direction of effect.}
The failure is systematic, not sampling noise. \emph{Every} one of the
$17{,}712$ candidate rules incurs a positive worst-case per-model accuracy drop
(minimum $1.85\pp$, median $20.1\pp$); \emph{zero} rules reach break-even. Across
all $637{,}632$ rule--environment evaluations, accuracy strictly \emph{falls} in
$67.7\%$ and strictly \emph{rises} in only $6.7\%$ (unchanged in the remaining
$25.5\%$), for a mean drop of $10.9\pp$. The rare gains are small and do not
concentrate in any rule or family. This is exactly the direction our mechanism
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}) predicts: consensus forms while the answer is still
moving, so acting on it removes correct recoveries more often than it banks a
settled trajectory.

Two claims should be kept separate. The \textbf{safety} result---no rule reaches
even $1.5\pp$, the floor being $1.85\pp$---is a statement about accuracy and, as
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} argues, is independent of how densely we probe. Only
the per-model condition is binding here; the per-benchmark cap is a diagnostic
(Section~\ref{sec:resolution}). This particular margin is thin: a bootstrap over
the nine benchmark$\times$seed environments puts the least-bad rule's worst-case
per-model drop at $1.85\pp$ with a $95\%$ CI of $[0.0,\,5.6]\pp$, which includes
break-even, so we do \emph{not} rest the result on that single rule's point
estimate. We rest it instead on the two facts that do not depend on it: all
$17{,}712$ rules lose accuracy (above), and even this least-bad rule spends
\emph{extra} tokens (its net savings are $-8$ to $-9\%$). Held-out confirmation
(C3) then tests whether the floor reproduces out of distribution. The
\textbf{savings} result---positive net savings costs $\ge 4.87\pp$---additionally
depends on our dense-probe accounting and is therefore the more caveated of the
two. Within the preregistered space one must choose between a stop that is
(nearly) safe but costs tokens and a stop that saves tokens but is unsafe.

\subsection{Family frontier}
Table~\ref{tab:frontier} gives each family's best attainable operating point.
The ordering is informative. The simplest evidence signal
(\texttt{window\_share}) gives the best safe-with-positive-savings point
($4.87\pp$); \texttt{entropy\_budget\_fraction} can push the drop down to
$1.85\pp$ but only by stopping so rarely that savings go negative; and
\texttt{adaptive\_event\_probe}---the most sophisticated family, with
entropy- and marker-triggered probing---is dominated, needing $9.70\pp$ for a
positive-savings point.

\subsection{Adaptive probing does not help}
Because event-triggered probing is the natural ``smarter'' fix, we isolate its
frontier (Table~\ref{tab:adaptive}). Even its most conservative point loses
$5.88\pp$ on average (worst environment $-9.70\pp$) for a $14.2\%$ mean saving;
becoming more aggressive trades linearly into larger losses ($-15.03\pp$ macro
for a $37.3\%$ saving). Entropy-drop and conclusion-marker triggers buy savings
only by paying accuracy---at its best positive-savings point the adaptive family
is dominated by simple window consensus. Sophistication in \emph{where} to probe
cannot fix the fact that the answer is still moving.

\subsection{Held-out confirmation}
We read the test split once (commitment~C3). Because the development result is
that \emph{no} rule clears the gate, there is no selected rule to confirm; we
instead ask whether the \emph{frontier} reproduces out of distribution, ranking
rules on dev and only \emph{measuring} them on test---never selecting there.

\paragraph{The frontier reproduces globally; the safe boundary is noise-limited.}
Across all $17{,}712$ rules, each rule's worst-case per-model accuracy drop on
test tracks its dev value (Pearson $r=0.96$), and every rule still loses in the
worst case. This global correlation is, however, dominated by dynamic range (dev
drops span $0$--$60\pp$): restricted toward the decision boundary it decays and
then vanishes ($r=0.65$ for dev drop $\le 10\pp$; $r\approx 0$ for $\le 5\pp$),
and the $144$ rules with dev drop $\le 3\pp$ all collapse to a single test value
($+0.11\pp$). The safe end is thus measurement-noise-limited on both splits---and
that is precisely the point: \textbf{no rule clears the conservative gate on both
splits} ($0$ on dev; of the $272$ rules admissible on test alone, not one is
admissible on dev---there they cost $4.98$--$5.65\pp$ per model, median $5.09$).
These $272$ are exactly the in-sample winners the held-out split exists to
reject: selecting directly on test would ``find'' safe rules that are not safe.

\paragraph{Unseen models.}
The same structure holds for models never used in development---the $32$B distill
(held-out scale) and Llama-8B (held-out architecture).\footnote{Llama-8B on
math500 and amc23; its $32{,}768$-token aime24 collection did not terminate for
this weaker model and is omitted.} On each, $100\%$ of rules lose in the worst
case and the dev-frontier rules deliver no net savings; no rule is jointly
safe-and-saving on any held-out model.

\paragraph{On the floor value.}
We report the $1.85\pp$ minimum as a \emph{development} quantity: the same
least-bad rule measures $0.11\pp$ on test (still with negative savings). The
split-invariant claims are therefore the empty joint gate and the collapse of
every near-safe dev rule to $\approx\!0\pp$ on test---not the exact floor, and
not a clean rank reproduction at the boundary, which is noise-limited on both
sides.
