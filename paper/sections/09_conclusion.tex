\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether intermediate answer-consensus is a safe signal on which to
early-exit a reasoning LLM. It is not, and not because the rule is untuned:
intermediate agreement measures answer stability under a fixed probe, not whether
reasoning has terminated---a \emph{stability--terminality gap}. Because continued
reasoning corrects the answer far more often than it breaks it, stopping on
consensus destroys a correct-in-the-end answer up to about $45$ times more often
(at an aggressive stop) than it rescues a wrong one. This recovery asymmetry is a
trajectory-level property, and it shows up as an empty safe-and-saving corner
across a preregistered space of $3{,}520$ rules---not one of which clears any
gate---reproduced on a held-out split and two unseen models, and paid in full by a
faithful Certaindex reproduction that collapses by $56$--$70\pp$. That the corner
is empty for consensus but not for early exit shows the failure is specific to the
\emph{signal}: swept through the identical protocol, a non-consensus method (DEER)
clears all three gates on the same trajectories. Our claim is therefore narrow and
firm---probe agreement \emph{alone} does not establish terminality and cannot be
tuned into a safe stop---while which signal \emph{should} replace it we leave open:
DEER shows a safe stop exists, not that it is the only or best one.
