\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether probe-based early exit for reasoning LLMs can be made safe by
choosing a better stopping rule. Across an intervention-free study on
\textsc{MATH}500 and a preregistered search over $17{,}712$ rules on two models
and three benchmarks, the answer is no for the family we search: intermediate
probe consensus is systematically non-terminal (a unanimous window is correct
only $93.5\%$ of the time; a naive stop loses $16.4\pp$), and on the development
set no rule in the searched space is both safe and token-saving (the safest
costs $1.85\pp$, positive net savings costs $\ge 4.87\pp$, and adaptive probing
is dominated by simple window consensus). Separating the intrinsic accuracy tax
from the reducible probe tax shows the accuracy floor is not a measurement
artifact. The practical takeaway is that safe early exit for reasoning likely
requires a different termination signal---one about the trajectory's future, not
the recent past's agreement---and held-out confirmation of this lower bound is
the immediate next step.
