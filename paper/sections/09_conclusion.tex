\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether self-consensus is a safe signal on which to
early-exit a reasoning LLM. Used on its own it is not: under a fixed probing
procedure agreement establishes that the
current answer \emph{persists}, not that the reasoning has \emph{terminated}---a
\emph{stability--terminality gap}. What the probes agree on is often a
placeholder the probe forced an unfinished trajectory to produce, so stopping on
it destroys far more correct-in-the-end answers than it rescues wrong ones, and
demanding more agreement only trades stopping opportunities away. That
asymmetry is a property of the trajectory, and it leaves the safe-and-saving
corner empty across a preregistered space of $3{,}520$ rules, while a
boundary-confidence control swept through the same protocol clears every gate.
Agreement fails not because it is insufficiently strict, but because it
repeatedly measures the wrong object.
