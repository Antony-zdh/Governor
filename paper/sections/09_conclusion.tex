\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether self-consensus is a safe signal on which to
early-exit a reasoning LLM. Used on its own it is not: under a fixed probing
procedure agreement establishes that the
current answer \emph{persists}, not that the reasoning has \emph{terminated}---a
\emph{stability--terminality gap}. What the probes agree on is often an answer
the model has not settled on---a placeholder the probe forced it to
produce---and stopping on it destroys a correct-in-the-end answer far more often
than it rescues a wrong one (up to ${\sim}45\times$ at an aggressive
latest-probe stop, ${\sim}2\times$ at the largest windows, where the rule has
nearly stopped firing), so demanding more agreement lowers the loss by firing
less often and only later in the trajectory. That asymmetry is a property of the
trajectory, and it leaves the safe-and-saving corner empty across the
preregistered space of $3{,}520$ rules---visible too at one shipped operating
point in CertaIndex (CoT)---while a boundary-confidence control swept through
the same protocol clears every gate. Agreement
fails not because it is insufficiently strict, but because it repeatedly
measures the wrong object.
