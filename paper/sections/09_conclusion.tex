\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether probe-based early exit for reasoning LLMs can be made safe by
choosing a better stopping rule. Across an intervention-free study on
\textsc{MATH}500 and a preregistered search over $17{,}712$ rules on two models
and three benchmarks, the answer is no: intermediate probe consensus is
systematically non-terminal (a unanimous window is correct only $93.5\%$ of the
time; naive stopping loses $16.4\pp$), and no rule in the space is both safe and
token-saving (the safest costs $1.85\pp$, positive net savings costs
$\ge 4.87\pp$, and adaptive probing is strictly worse than simple window
consensus). Separating the intrinsic accuracy tax from the reducible probe tax
shows the accuracy floor is not a measurement artifact. The practical takeaway is
that safe early exit for reasoning requires a different termination
signal---one about the trajectory's future, not the recent past's agreement.
