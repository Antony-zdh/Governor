\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether intermediate answer-consensus is a safe signal on which to
early-exit a reasoning LLM. It is not, and not because the rule is untuned:
intermediate consensus forms while the answer is still moving, so stopping on it
destroys a correct-in-the-end answer about $35$ times more often than it rescues
a wrong one. This accuracy tax is a property of the trajectory rather than the
probe, and it shows up as an empty safe-and-saving corner across a preregistered
space of $17{,}712$ rules---every one of which loses accuracy---reproduced on a
held-out split and two unseen models, and paid in full by a faithful Certaindex
reproduction that collapses by $56$--$70\pp$. Because a method reading a
non-consensus signal stays near full accuracy on the very same trajectories, the
failure is specific to consensus. The takeaway for anyone building safe early
exit is therefore about the choice of signal: read the model's own
forward-looking state, not the agreement of its backward-looking probes.
