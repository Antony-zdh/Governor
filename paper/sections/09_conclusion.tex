\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether intermediate answer-consensus is a safe signal on which to
early-exit a reasoning LLM. Used on its own it is not, and not because the rule is
untuned: under a fixed probing procedure agreement establishes that the current
answer \emph{persists}, not that the reasoning has \emph{terminated}---a
\emph{stability--terminality gap}. What the probes agree on is often an answer the
model has not settled on---three in five of our hand-labelled wrong stops commit
to a placeholder the probe forced it to produce, and re-wording the probe changes
the early answer more often than not---because a probe forces an output rather
than reading a finished belief. Stopping on such an answer destroys a
correct-in-the-end answer far more often than it rescues a wrong one (up to
${\sim}45\times$ at an aggressive latest-probe stop, ${\sim}2\times$ at the largest
windows, where the rule has nearly stopped firing), so demanding more agreement
lowers the loss only by declining to fire. The recovery asymmetry behind this is a
property of the trajectory, and it shows up as an empty safe-and-saving corner
across a preregistered space of $3{,}520$ rules---not one of which clears any
gate---reproduced on a held-out split and two unseen models, and visible at one
shipped operating point in CertaIndex (CoT), which loses $56$--$70\pp$. That the
corner is empty for consensus but not for early exit is a positive control, not a
proposal: swept through the identical protocol, a method reading the model's own
boundary confidence (DEER) clears all three gates on the very same trajectories,
so the failure is specific to consensus rather than to early exit. This does not
put probe agreement beyond use---it is cheap, and it does report that the current
answer has not changed; what our results rule out is treating that report, by
itself, as evidence that the reasoning is done. Repeated agreement under a fixed
probing procedure establishes answer persistence, not reasoning terminality;
within the broad preregistered family studied here, making that agreement stricter
only trades away stopping opportunities rather than turning it into a safe stopping
signal. Agreement fails not because it is insufficiently strict, but because it
repeatedly measures the wrong object.
