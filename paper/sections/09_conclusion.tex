\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Intermediate answer-consensus is not a safe early-exit signal for reasoning LLMs,
and not because the rule is untuned: agreement measures answer stability under a
fixed probe, not whether reasoning has terminated---a \emph{stability--terminality
gap}. Because continued reasoning corrects the answer far more often than it breaks
it, stopping on consensus destroys a correct-in-the-end answer up to $45$
times more often than it banks one. This trajectory-level recovery
asymmetry leaves the safe-and-saving corner empty across all $3{,}520$
rules, reproduced on a held-out split and two unseen models, whereas a
non-consensus signal (DEER) swept identically clears every gate, localizing the
failure to the \emph{signal}. Probe agreement \emph{alone} does not establish
terminality and cannot be tuned into a safe stop; which signal \emph{should} replace
it we leave open.
