\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Section~\ref{sec:results} is an existence proof over $3{,}520$ rules, two seen
and two unseen models, and both splits. This section explains \emph{why} it holds
and why it generalizes beyond the exact rules we searched. The one-line reason is
a \emph{stability--terminality gap}: probe agreement measures whether the current
answer is \emph{stable}, not whether the reasoning has \emph{terminated}, and that
gap is a directional property of the trajectory itself---not of a threshold or of
probe density.

\subsection{The accuracy tax is intrinsic to the trajectory}
Count, among stops that \emph{change} correctness relative to the trajectory's own
final answer, a correct recovery destroyed (final-correct, stop-wrong) versus a
wrong answer luckily banked (final-wrong, stop-correct). Sampling noise predicts
$\approx\!1{:}1$; a ``continued reasoning corrects'' effect predicts $\gg\!1$. On
the frozen replay this ratio is window-dependent---$\approx\!45{:}1$ at a
latest-probe stop ($W{=}1$) down to $\approx\!2{:}1$ at $W{=}30$---but the low end
is bought only by stopping so rarely that net saving vanishes ($W{=}30$ fires on
$121$ dev problems vs.\ $668$ at $W{=}1$). At any window that saves tokens the
ratio stays well above $1{:}1$: consensus destroys a correct-in-the-end answer many
times more often than it banks a wrong one---the recovery phenomenon of
\S\ref{sec:exp-fc} ($84.2\%$, $89.1\%$) as a signed effect at the main budgets.
This is structural: a rule stopping at position $s$ forgoes whatever correction the
trajectory makes after $s$, a property of the reasoning, not the probe, so it
survives \emph{any} probing scheme and never reaches zero in the sweep. The probe
grid ($64/128/256/512$) makes essentially every stop position reachable; a sparser
schedule can only stop later, never at a safer position the grid missed. So
agreement reports answer \emph{stability}---what the model said recently---not
whether reasoning has \emph{terminated}. DEER, by contrast, holds
$\approx\!2.4$--$3.5{:}1$ \emph{while} saving $28$--$32\%$, committing mainly once
the trajectory has settled.

\subsection{The probe reads stability, not terminality}
\label{sec:two-wording}
Two direct measurements make the gap concrete from opposite sides.
\textbf{Probe reads are wording-unstable early.} We re-probe the \emph{same}
frozen prefixes with two differently worded answer queries---the \simplek{} suffix
and a Certaindex-style suffix---and measure how often they extract the \emph{same}
current answer (\dsseven{}, \textsc{MATH}500; $2{,}898$ probe points binned by
trajectory position; Table~\ref{tab:twowording}). Agreement rises monotonically:
the two wordings disagree on the current answer ${\sim}57\%$ of the time early in a
trajectory (only $41\%$ agree in the first $5\%$), but just $11\%$ near its end
($89\%$ agree). Early on, the ``answer'' a probe surfaces is an artifact of how it
is asked as much as of the underlying reasoning---the same prefix probed two ways
yields two answers---and the wordings converge only as the trajectory settles.
Agreement therefore certifies that the current answer is \emph{stable to how we
ask}, not that reasoning has finished.
\textbf{Stopped-but-wrong answers are mostly non-terminal.} A two-annotator study
of stopped-but-wrong cases (\S\ref{sec:exp-fc}; Appendix~\ref{sec:fc-taxonomy})
shows the same gap from the error side: $61\%$ of stopped-but-wrong commitments
are to answers the trajectory had \emph{not yet converged} on (type~D), versus
only ${\sim}18\%$ that are settled but wrong (type~A). Both say the same thing:
probe agreement is a stability signal, and stability is reached well before
terminality.

\subsection{It is the signal---not dense probing, not early exit}
\label{sec:signal}
Two objections remain; dismissing both localizes the failure to the signal.
\emph{``It is just dense probing.''} Besides the accuracy tax, a rule pays a
\emph{probe tax}: the probe output $p$ (Section~\ref{sec:accounting}). Under dense
re-probing a late-stopping large-window rule pays $p$ over a long prefix, opening a
gap between its \emph{gross} and \emph{net} savings (Table~\ref{tab:grossnet}).
But the probe tax scales with probe density and is reducible (sparser or shorter
probes, KV-cache reuse), whereas the accuracy floor is probe-independent; it is
the accuracy floor, not the probe tax, that keeps the corner empty---and it is the
accuracy floor that DEER escapes. \emph{``Maybe early exit itself is
impossible.''} It is not. Every method that stops on \emph{probe agreement}---the
swept consensus family and CertaIndex alike---either fails a gate or collapses
(CertaIndex loses $70\pp$ at a $99.8\%$ stop rate, the accuracy tax paid in full).
The one method that clears the gates, DEER, does not read probe agreement at all:
it stops on the model's confidence in a trial answer at a reasoning boundary, and
in Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} its threshold frontier sits \emph{above} the consensus
Pareto boundary throughout the safe region. Because DEER is swept through the
identical pipeline, gates, and token accounting, the contrast is a controlled
test: the same early-exit machinery succeeds on a forward-looking signal and fails
on the consensus signal. The failure is a property of the \emph{signal}, not of
early exit.
