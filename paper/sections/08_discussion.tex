\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the negative result does and does not say.}
It says: within a large, preregistered space of \emph{consensus-based} stopping
rules, and under a dense-probe measurement regime, no rule on the development
set is both safe (per-model drop $\le 1.5\pp$) and net token-saving. It does
\emph{not} say early exit is impossible in principle, nor that it is impossible
outside the searched schema. The result localizes the failure: the signal these
rules consume---agreement among probes of an unfinished trajectory---is
intrinsically non-terminal, so no threshold on that signal escapes the accuracy
tax.

\paragraph{Implication: change the signal, not the threshold.}
Much prior effort has gone into \emph{tuning} consensus-based stops
\citep{dynasor_certaindex,aggarwal2023adaptive,li2024esc}. Our frontier shows the
ceiling of that approach is low, and Section~\ref{sec:boundary} gives direct
evidence that a different termination signal---one about whether the
\emph{trajectory itself} will still change---does better: boundary confidence
with a verification branch saves $\sim\!44\%$ of tokens at neutral accuracy.
Other candidates point the same way: verifier- or process-reward-based
termination \citep{lightman2024verify,wang2024mathshepherd} and learned halting
in the spirit of adaptive computation \citep{graves2016act,schuster2022confident}.
The lesson is not that early exit is hopeless but that it should read the
model's own forward-looking confidence, not the agreement of backward-looking
probes.

\paragraph{Confirmation done; two live extensions.}
The held-out confirmation (Section~\ref{sec:results}) already validates the
frontier out of distribution: the per-rule frontier reproduces on the test split
($r=0.96$) and no rule clears the gate on both dev and test, on the two seen and
two unseen models alike. Two follow-ups remain, both compatible with the
preregistration. \textbf{(A)} \emph{Attacking the probe tax}: a newly
preregistered extension with sparser probes / KV-cache reuse could recover
positive \emph{net} savings at the safe end, converting ``no safe-and-saving stop
in the searched space'' into ``\ldots exists only if probing is made this much
cheaper.'' \textbf{(B)} \emph{Validating boundary confidence}: the online
controller of Section~\ref{sec:boundary} is single-seed and model-dependent
($+2.8\pp$ on \qweneight{} but $-3.5\pp$ on \dsseven{}); a multi-seed
train/dev$\rightarrow$test evaluation would turn the positive signal into a
confirmed method. Both are additive to the frozen findings of
Sections~\ref{sec:falseconsensus}--\ref{sec:mechanism}.
