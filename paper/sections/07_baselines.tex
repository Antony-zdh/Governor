\section{It Is the Signal, Not Early Exit}
\label{sec:baselines}

Two questions remain: does the accuracy tax reach \emph{published} systems, and
is it specific to the consensus signal or a fact about early exit in general? We
answer both by reproducing three prior early-stoppers in a common harness---the
same frozen trajectories, the same \simplek{} probe bank, the same token
accounting---and by confirming the frontier out of distribution.

\subsection{Prior stoppers on the same trajectories}
\textbf{CertaIndex} \citep{dynasor_certaindex} is the faithful share-threshold
stop, the direct descendant of the swept consensus family. \textbf{DEER}
\citep{deer} stops at reasoning-boundary (``\texttt{Wait}'') tokens on the
model's \emph{own} confidence in a short trial answer---a different signal from
probe agreement. \textbf{TJE} \citep{tje} is a token-level early exit. All
numbers are dev macro-averages over three benchmarks per model ($18$
environments), reproductions on our frozen trajectories rather than the original
papers' end-to-end runs.

Table~\ref{tab:baselines} sharpens the negative result and locates its cause.
Every method that stops on \emph{probe agreement}---the swept schema and
CertaIndex alike---either fails the safety bar or, in Certaindex's case,
collapses outright: at $99.8\%$ stop rate and $70\pp$ lost, it is the accuracy
tax paid in full. The lone method that stays near full accuracy, DEER, does not
read probe agreement at all; in Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} it sits in the low-drop
corner, \emph{above} the consensus Pareto boundary at the same drop. This is
direct evidence that the failure is a property of the \emph{consensus signal},
not of early exit. (We caution that DEER's edge is model-dependent, $+0.8\pp$ on
\qweneight{} vs.\ $-4.8\pp$ on \dsseven{}, and these are frozen-trajectory
reproductions; how far a non-consensus signal can actually be pushed is an
exploratory question we take up in Appendix~\ref{sec:boundary}.)

\subsection{The frontier reproduces out of distribution}
We read the test split once (commitment~C3). Because the development result is
that \emph{no} rule clears the gate, there is nothing to confirm by re-selection;
we instead ask whether the \emph{frontier} reproduces, ranking rules on dev and
only measuring them on test.

\paragraph{Held-out test split.}
Across all $17{,}712$ rules, each rule's worst-case per-model drop on test tracks
its dev value (Pearson $r=0.96$), and every rule still loses in the worst case.
This global correlation is dominated by dynamic range (dev drops span
$0$--$60\pp$); toward the decision boundary it is noise-limited on both sides
($r\approx0$ for dev drop $\le5\pp$; the $144$ rules with dev drop $\le3\pp$
collapse to a single test value, $+0.11\pp$). That is precisely the point:
\textbf{no rule clears the conservative gate on both splits}. There are $0$ on
dev, and of the $272$ rules admissible on \emph{test alone}, not one is
admissible on dev---there they cost $4.98$--$5.65\pp$ per model (median $5.09$).
These $272$ are exactly the in-sample winners the held-out split exists to
reject.

\paragraph{Unseen scale ($4\times$).}
On the \mbox{Distill-Qwen-32B} model, never used in development, the same
structure holds: the per-rule dev$\rightarrow$test frontier reproduces
($r=0.903$), and the dev-selected ``safe'' rules do not transfer---rules with
dev drop $\le1.5\pp$ cost a mean $+2.58\pp$ and up to $+8.33\pp$ on the held-out
test split, and the dev floor rule itself drops $+8.33\pp$ there. Scaling the
model $4\times$ does not open a safe corner; the dev-in-sample safety is
overfitting, and the negative result holds.

\paragraph{Unseen architecture.}
The held-out architecture transfer (\mbox{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B})
reproduces the same qualitative pattern---the dev-frontier rules deliver no net
savings and every rule loses in the worst case.\footnote{The Llama-8B
trajectories were re-collected after correcting a chat-template defect in the
earlier run (a missing beginning-of-sequence token specific to this model's
tokenizer that degraded its generations); the corrected model scores
$\sim\!88\%$ on \textsc{MATH}500. Full corrected numbers are in the released
sweep.} Across the held-out test split and both unseen models, no rule is jointly
safe-and-saving.
