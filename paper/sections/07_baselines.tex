\section{Prior Early-Stoppers on the Same Trajectories}
\label{sec:baselines}

Our sweep searches \emph{consensus}-based rules. To place prior work on the same
footing---and to test whether the failure is specific to the consensus
signal---we reproduce three published early-stoppers in a common harness: the
same frozen trajectories, the same \simplek{} probe bank, and the same token
accounting (probe output tokens included). All numbers are dev macro-averages
over three benchmarks per model ($18$ environments); they are reproductions on
our frozen trajectories, not the original papers' end-to-end results.

\paragraph{Methods.}
\textbf{CertaIndex} \citep{dynasor_certaindex}: the faithful Certaindex
share-threshold stop---the direct descendant of the consensus family we sweep.
\textbf{DEER} \citep{deer}: stops at reasoning-boundary (``\texttt{Wait}'')
tokens using the model's \emph{own} confidence in a short trial answer---a
different signal from probe agreement. \textbf{TJE} \citep{tje}: a token-level
early-exit stop.

\paragraph{The consensus signal is the problem, not early exit.}
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} sharpens the negative result. Every method that stops
on \emph{probe agreement}---the swept schema and CertaIndex alike---either fails
the safety bar or (CertaIndex) collapses outright. The lone method that stays
near full accuracy, DEER, does not use probe agreement at all: it stops on the
model's confidence in a trial answer emitted at a reasoning boundary. This is
the first direct evidence that the failure we characterize is a property of the
\emph{consensus signal}, and it motivates asking how far a boundary-confidence
signal can be pushed (Section~\ref{sec:boundary}). We caution that DEER's
advantage is model-dependent ($+0.8\pp$ on \qweneight{} but $-4.8\pp$ on
\dsseven{}) and that these are frozen-trajectory reproductions.
