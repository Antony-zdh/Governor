\begin{abstract}
Reasoning language models spend most of their tokens \emph{after} the answer is
effectively decided, so a popular way to cut inference cost is to periodically
probe the partial trajectory for its current answer and stop once probes
\emph{agree}. We show this bet is unsafe, and that the failure is a property of
the \emph{signal}, not of the stopping threshold. On frozen reasoning
trajectories from two models and three math benchmarks we run a
\emph{preregistered} sweep of the consensus signal---a window size $\times$
share threshold family plus operational knobs, $3{,}520$ rules---with
problem-grouped splits and acceptance gates (a total accuracy-drop cap, a total
token-saving floor, and a positive-saving fraction) fixed in advance. The result
is distribution-free: \textbf{not one of the $3{,}520$ rules clears any gate};
capping the accuracy drop at $1\pp$ forces net saving to near zero, while
demanding a $10\%$ saving forces a multi-point drop, and even large windows only
trade the drop for zero saving. The reason is directional, not statistical:
intermediate consensus forms \emph{while the answer is still moving}, so a stop
destroys a correct-in-the-end answer roughly $\mathbf{35}$~\textbf{times more
often} than it rescues a wrong one---an \emph{accuracy tax} intrinsic to the
trajectory. The failure is specific to \emph{consensus}: swept through the
identical pipeline, gates, and token accounting, a method reading a different
signal---the model's own confidence at reasoning-boundary tokens (DEER)---
\textbf{clears all three gates}, losing as little as $0.3\pp$ while saving $28\%$.
The consensus frontier reproduces on a held-out test split ($r{=}0.98$) and on
unseen models at $4\times$ the scale and a different architecture, with no
consensus rule safe on both splits, while DEER stays admissible; a faithful
Certaindex reproduction on identical trajectories collapses by $56$--$70\pp$.
Early exit is possible---just not on the consensus signal. We release the
protocol, the frozen trajectories, and the full sweep.
\end{abstract}
