\section{A Signal That Works: Boundary-Confidence Early Exit}
\label{sec:boundary}

Section~\ref{sec:baselines} showed that the one prior method staying near full
accuracy, DEER, reads a different signal. We now push that signal into a
deployment-style online controller and find it recovers substantial savings at
neutral accuracy---the constructive counterpart to our negative result. These
results are exploratory (single seed, outside the preregistered sweep); we report
them to establish that the failure is about the \emph{signal}, not early exit.

\subsection{From trial-answer confidence to a verification branch}
DEER emits a short trial answer at reasoning-boundary (``\texttt{Wait}'')
tokens and stops once the model's confidence in that trial answer (the mean
probability of its answer tokens) exceeds a threshold. On our development
trajectories the mechanism has a flaw: DEER measures confidence on the trial
answer but then \emph{delivers a separately generated readout}, and the two
disagree on $14.6\%$ of triggered problems. The extra readout averages $470$
tokens yet yields no net accuracy gain (trial $88.9\%$ vs.\ readout $88.5\%$),
though it corrects some cases on some models. We therefore evaluate an
\emph{inspired} controller that (i) \emph{fast-commits} the trial answer when
confidence is very high ($>\!0.995$), skipping the redundant readout, and (ii)
otherwise opens a \emph{retained verification branch} that commits only if a
second high-confidence answer is mathematically equivalent, and otherwise keeps
reasoning. Both run in a single online engine that generates the main trajectory
live and probes only at scheduled boundaries---no frozen suffixes.

\subsection{Online deployment results}
Table~\ref{tab:boundary} reports an online run over the $18$-environment
development set (seed $42$; $456$ trajectories). The inspired controller holds
macro accuracy essentially flat ($-0.36\pp$; $+2.78\pp$ on \qweneight{}) while
saving $\sim\!44\%$ of tokens, with $69\%$ of problems fast-committed and $15\%$
routed through a verification branch. Against a faithful online DEER reference,
a stratified paired bootstrap ($10{,}000$ resamples) gives an accuracy
difference of $+0.97\pp$ (95\% CI $[-10.5, +12.6]$) and a token-saving advantage
of $+15.3\%$ (95\% CI $[+7.3, +22.9]$): the controller saves significantly more
at indistinguishable accuracy.

\subsection{Relation to the preregistration}
This controller is \emph{not} part of the preregistered consensus sweep: it uses
a different signal, a separate configuration, and its own results directory, and
it would require its own train/dev$\rightarrow$test validation before any
confirmed claim. It does not modify the sweep's candidates, gates, or selected
rules (there were none). We therefore present it as a positive \emph{signal},
not a finished method: it shows that stepping outside the consensus-signal space
escapes the accuracy tax that Section~\ref{sec:results} proved unavoidable
inside it.
